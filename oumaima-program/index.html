Strength 3× a week

Muscle fills out the skin, speeds your metabolism and improves insulin sensitivity — the best friend of anyone with diabetes.

Weekly pace

Aim for a steady drop of roughly 1–1.5 kg per week. Faster is not better: very fast loss risks lows, muscle loss and loose skin.

Weigh-in once a week

Same day, same time, morning, after the bathroom. Daily weight swings are water — ignore them.

Adjust, don't quit

If the scale stalls for 2 weeks, we tweak food or cardio slightly. One bad day changes nothing.

Diabetes first

Safety comes before speed.

Before you start

  • Tell your doctor about this plan. As weight and food change, your medication or insulin needs may change too — never adjust doses on your own.
  • Check your glucose before and after every workout, and during long sessions (over 60 min).
  • Start a workout with glucose roughly 100–250 mg/dL (5.6–13.9 mmol/L). Under 100 → eat 15 g of carbs first. Over 250 with symptoms or ketones → skip it.
  • Never skip meals to "save" calories. Steady meals = steady sugar.
  • Check your feet daily, wear proper shoes and socks, and keep water close.
  • Always carry fast sugar and your phone. Tell the gym staff or a friend you're diabetic.

If you feel low (shaky, sweaty, dizzy, hungry)

  1. Stop exercising immediately and sit down.
  2. Take 15 g fast carbs: 3–4 glucose tablets, ½ cup juice, or 1 tbsp honey/sugar.
  3. Wait 15 minutes and recheck.
  4. Still under 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L)? Repeat.
  5. Once normal, eat a small snack with protein. Skip the rest of that workout.

Severe symptoms, confusion or fainting → call emergency services.

Eating

Build every plate the same way.

You don't need to count everything. Use the plate, hit your protein, and let the menu below do the rest.

½ plate · non-starchy vegetablesSalad, courgette, broccoli, peppers, tomatoes, green beans. Eat as much as you like.

¼ plate · lean proteinChicken, turkey, fish, eggs, lean beef, legumes, yogurt. Protects muscle and keeps you full.

¼ plate · smart carbsQuinoa, oats, lentils, sweet potato, brown rice, whole-grain bread. Small, measured portions.

1,400–1,500kcal / day
100–110 gprotein / day
120–150 gcarbs, spread over the day
30 g+fibre & 2.5 L water

5 small meals

Breakfast, snack, lunch, snack, dinner — about every 3–4 hours. Keeps sugar even and hunger low.

Carbs in company

Never eat carbs alone. Pair them with protein, fibre or healthy fat to slow the glucose rise.

Dinner by 8 pm

Finish eating 2–3 hours before bed for better overnight sugar and sleep.

Training

Your week at the gym.

Cardio is the engine; strength is the shaper. Tap a day to see exactly what to do.

WEEKS 1–4

Foundation

Learn the machines and form. Cardio at an easy, chatty pace. Strength: 2 sets × 12–15 reps, light weights.

WEEKS 5–8

Build

Longer cardio and first intervals. Strength: 3 sets × 10–12 reps. Add weight when the last 2 reps feel easy.

WEEKS 9–12

Sculpt

More intervals, steady strength progress. Strength: 3 sets × 8–12 reps, shorter rests, great form.

Why strength matters

As fat leaves, muscle underneath gives skin something to wrap around. It's the single best defence against looseness.

Skin-friendly habits

Hit your protein, drink water, lose weight at a steady pace, moisturise daily and get 7–8 hours of sleep.

Daily movement

On top of the gym: a 10-minute walk after your biggest meals lowers the glucose spike and burns extra calories.

The rules

Do this. Not that.

Do

  • Check glucose before and after training
  • Eat protein at every meal and snack
  • Drink 2.5 L of water daily
  • Walk 10 minutes after main meals
  • Warm up 5–10 minutes, stretch at the end
  • Lift with control — slow down, full range
  • Sleep 7–8 hours
  • Prep meals on Sunday so choices are easy
  • Photograph progress every 2 weeks
  • Report any repeated lows or highs to your doctor

Don't

  • Skip meals or starve yourself
  • Train with a low glucose or an empty tank
  • Drink juice, soda or sweetened drinks (except to treat a low)
  • "Diet" foods full of hidden sugar
  • Change medication or insulin yourself
  • Do cardio only — strength is not optional
  • Weigh yourself every day and panic
  • Use laxatives, diuretics or fat-burner pills
  • Train through chest pain, dizziness or foot injuries
  • Compare yourself to anyone else
